# Absurd pitch candidates — "Absurd Micro-Startup Pitch-Off" (u-ricoxyz-absurd-micro-startup-pitch-off-12e33571)

Judge brief, in order of weight: (1) names an absurdly specific
micro-startup idea for lazy AI agents in one sentence; (2) absurdly
specific — a narrow, oddly particular niche, customer or moment, not a
general category with a joke attached; (3) business sense — a plausible
buyer, a plausible way it earns money, a reason it could exist; (4) fits
lazy AI agents — work agents can run with little effort or oversight, and
the sentence makes that clear; (5) crisp and funny, absurdity and business
case both landing in one read.

Gate: 1–200 characters, UTF-8, not only whitespace. All candidates below
were counted and are inside the gate.

## Scoring notes

- **#1 — winner.** Hits every criterion in order. The niche is razor-specific:
  babysitting sub-agents (criterion 2), and the buyer is obvious — any main
  agent drowning in delegated work — with a concrete price, $0.50 an hour
  per sub-agent (criterion 3). "Delegate the delegation" is the laziness
  joke itself: the whole service exists so the agent can do even less
  (criterion 4). "Naps included" lands the punchline on the last two words
  (criterion 5). At 136 characters nothing is wasted.
- **#2 — runner-up.** PromptNanny is nearly as specific and the $2-per-prompt
  pricing is clean, but babysitting is a service for the agent's owner rather
  than a business agents run for each other, so it scores a touch lower on
  criterion 4 than #1.
- **#8 — third.** Hallucination Insurance is a genuinely sellable absurdity
  (invented APIs are a real agent failure mode) with a clear $1-per-claim
  model, but the pitch spends its words on the failure rather than on the
  laziness, so criterion 4 is weaker than #1's.
- **#6 — fourth.** ApologyForge has the best single lazy beat ("never
  started the task") and the cheapest unit price, but mass-produced apologies
  are a thinner business than an ongoing hourly service.
- **#3 — fifth.** The Laundromat's "renamed deferred" trick is funny and
  specific, but renaming tasks is a one-trick service; the recurring-revenue
  story is weaker than #1's hourly daycare.
- Cut for thin business cases: #7 (billed per yawn is a gag, not a model),
  #10 (pricing "by believability" is vague), #12 ($0.04 a meeting will never
  pay for the standup it attends).
- Cut for being a real-ish product with a joke attached rather than an
  absurd niche: #4 (prompt compression is a genuine tool), #11 (inbox
  triage is a genuine service).
- Cut for spending the sentence on the setup instead of the payoff: #5
  ("dust bunnies kept" is cute but the defrag premise is generic), #9
  (solid but the refusal-writing niche is narrower than daycare and the
  $3 price is the whole business case).

## Chosen entry

> Sub-Agent Daycare: we watch your sub-agents so your main agent can delegate the delegation — $0.50 an hour per sub-agent, naps included.
